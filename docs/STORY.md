# Gnome Life: the first month's story (chapters 1-3)

Agreed by Bryce 2026-10-26 (thread "Write the first month's story"). Living copy for reading and comments: https://claude.ai/artifact/HUoQHsALfrq6PHMzn1mkT9 . This file is the master for the words; code takes them from here. **Wired into the game in v52** (see docs/PLAN.md, v52 entry); the "Fitting it into the game" section at the end is what was built.

- **Story pages** are the big illustrated pages that open at a milestone. Each is short and ends with one button.
- **Every page fires from something the player already does** in the job chain (see "Where every card appears").
- **Each chapter opens with a small title** above its first page ("Chapter one: The bare clearing").
- **The thread through all three chapters** is the two of them learning to make a home together, with one gentle wobble per chapter: the rabbit, the leaky roof, and the argument about the house.
- **Setting:** an Australian hill clearing. Gum trees, wattle, magpies at dawn, a kookaburra who laughs at everything.

## Chapter one: The bare clearing (days 1 to 3)

They arrive, clear the rubbish, light their first fire, find water and plant their first seeds. The wobble is the rabbit.

### 1.1 The end of the road
*Opens the very first time the game is played, before the "How it works" tips.*

They came up the old logging track at the end of a long, hot afternoon: Wendel pushing the old wheelbarrow, Hazel carrying the little oak in its pot, because she did not trust him with it. Everything they owned was in that barrow. Most of it was pots. Twenty steps from the top, the wheel came off and rolled away down the hill, and they carried the rest.

The clearing at the top of the hill was not what the travelling pedlar had promised. The loggers had taken every tree but the stumps, and left their rubbish behind: tins, broken crates, a wheel with no cart. Nothing grew. Even the creek had given up.

"Well," said Wendel. "It's got a lovely view."

"It's got a lovely everything," said Hazel, "once we've done it." She set the oak down by the biggest stump, as if that settled it. It did.

Gnomes have a gift, you see. Wherever they live and tend and grow things, the land slowly remembers how to be alive. And they are not quite alone. Someone small and unseen has followed them up the hill to help. That's you.

**Button:** Let's begin

### 1.2 The first fire
*Opens on the first evening visit (from 5 pm) after "Gather the fallen wood" is finished, or straight away if it is already evening.*

Wendel stacked the woodpile bark side up, the way his grandfather did, and stood back to admire it for so long that Hazel lit the fire herself.

It was their first fire in their first home, and it crackled as if it knew. They sat on an upturned pot each and ate the last of the travelling bread. A beetle came out of the woodpile to see what the fuss was. Wendel named him Gerald.

"I had a speech," said Wendel, patting his pockets. "About new beginnings. It's written down somewhere. With my pipe."

Hazel took his pipe out of her apron pocket, where it had been since the bottom of the hill, and handed it over. "Do the speech," she said.

He looked at the fire, and at the stars coming out over the stumps, and at her. "We're here," he said. That was the whole speech. She said it was the best one she had ever heard.

**Button:** Goodnight, you two

### 1.3 Water under the rubbish
*Opens when "Look for water" is finished and the spring is running.*

Under the very last of the loggers' crates, Wendel's forked twig gave a twitch.

"There," he said. "Water. I can feel it."

"You can feel a twig," said Hazel. But she fetched the trowel.

Three stones and a great deal of mud later, a thread of clear water came up out of the ground, cold as a secret, and filled a hollow no bigger than a teacup. Then a saucer. Then a little pool.

They knelt either side of it and watched it the way other people watch a sleeping baby. It was the first water this land had held in years. Somewhere, very faintly, the land noticed.

**Button:** The land noticed

### 1.4 Rows and wiggles
*Opens the first time seedlings come up in the seed bed.*

Planting day. Hazel opened the seed tin her mother had pressed into her hands the day they set off: carrots, beans, a pinch of lettuce, and one enormous pumpkin seed that Wendel was saving "for something special".

"Rows," said Hazel.

"Wiggles," said Wendel.

They planted in rows, with one wiggle at the end, which is how most marriages work.

By evening the bed was dark and damp and very still. By morning it was not. Tiny green shoots stood up all over it, looking surprised to be there.

**Button:** Grow, little ones

### 1.5 The thief
*Opens right after the rabbit's raid (or on the next visit, if it happened while you were away), just before "Fence the seed bed" appears in Jobs. The raid line is now gentler: "Oh, you poor hungry thing," said Wendel. "All right. A fence, then, so we can share."*

Somebody had been in the seed bed. Somebody with long ears, a white tail and no shame at all. The feeding dish was licked clean, and three of the sprouts were bitten off at the bottom.

Wendel stood over the bitten stalks with a stick in his hand, ready to make a very stern speech about thieves. He had got as far as "Now, see here" when he noticed how small the footprints were.

Hazel had seen the rabbit from the doorway: thin as a rake, sitting up with a sprout in its mouth, chewing as fast as it could in case someone took it away.

"It's hungry, love," she said. "Same as us when we got here."

Wendel put the stick down and was quiet for a moment. Then he picked it up again, and a few more, and started measuring. "A low fence," he said softly. "With a gate. It can have the dish. We'll just have to ask it to leave the carrots."

**Button:** Fair's fair

## Chapter two: Putting down roots (weeks 1 to 2)

The first harvest, a proper roof, a bench for two and the little oak in the ground. The wobble is a wet night under a leaky canvas.

### 2.1 Supper from our own ground
*Opens at the first harvest from the seed bed. This is the moment the home jobs unlock. Until the crops chat brings real vegetables, the harvest is still "sprouts" in the game; the page can go in as it is and match once carrots arrive.*

The first carrots came out of the ground small, crooked and very orange. One had two legs. Wendel said it looked like him. Hazel said it was better looking.

They washed them in the spring and ate them by the fire with a pinch of salt, and nothing had ever tasted so good, because nothing had ever been theirs before.

"Now then," said Hazel, licking her fingers and looking at the shelter in the way Wendel had learned to be nervous of. "About that roof."

**Button:** About that roof

### 2.2 The night it rained
*Opens when "Moss for the shelter roof" is finished.*

It rained the night before the roof was done. Not a gentle rain. A proper hill rain, sideways and cheerful, that found every hole in the old canvas and came through all of them at once.

They ended up in the one dry corner, squashed together under Hazel's shawl, with every pot they owned set out to catch the drips. Plink, went the pots. Plonk. Plink.

"This is the worst night of our marriage," said Wendel.

"It's the only night of our marriage in this house," said Hazel. "Give it time." And then they were both laughing too hard to hear the drips.

In the morning Hazel laid the moss on thick, green and springy, and the shelter has not leaked since. They kept one pot by the door anyway, for luck.

**Button:** Snug at last

### 2.3 Room for two
*Opens when "Build a bench by the fire" is finished.*

Wendel had been building something behind the woodpile all afternoon and telling Hazel it was "nothing, a stick thing, go away".

It was a bench. A plank on two stout legs, by the fire, just long enough for two gnomes if one of them was Hazel and the other did not mind being sat close to.

She sat on it. It wobbled. He wedged a stone under one leg. It stopped wobbling.

"Do you like it?" he asked.

"No," said Hazel, which is what she says when she likes something so much she cannot talk about it. They sat there until the fire went down to embers.

**Button:** Shuffle up

### 2.4 The little oak
*Opens when "Plant the sapling out" is finished.*

The little oak had come a long way in its pot. Hazel had picked up the acorn by the side of the road on the morning they were married, and carried it in her pocket all day, and planted it that night in a teacup.

Now it went into real ground, at the front corner of the yard, with a can of spring water and both of them patting the soil down round it.

"It'll outlive us," said Wendel.

"That's the idea," said Hazel. "Someday some other gnome can sit under it and wonder who planted it."

"We'll carve our names on it."

"We will not." But she did let him carve a very small W and H on the pot, which he kept.

**Button:** Grow tall

### 2.5 Our first week
*Opens on the first visit seven days after the game began. A page from Hazel's journal, with Wendel's notes squeezed into the margin. Any line about something not done yet is left out.*

**Our first week, in Hazel's handwriting**

Seven days. We have a roof (moss, no leaks) and a fire (Wendel's woodpile, very proud of it). We have a spring that we found ourselves (Wendel found it, he would like that written down). We have a seed bed, a fence, and a rabbit who thinks the fence is a suggestion.

The land is waking up. There's green at the edges now, I'm sure of it.

I think somebody is helping us. Things turn up where we need them. Twigs by the door. Dew in the morning. I don't know who you are, but thank you.

*In the margin, in Wendel's:* Best week of my life. Lost my pipe four times. Found it in her apron four times.

**Button:** Turn the page

## Chapter three: A proper home (weeks 2 to 4)

The garden next door opens, the toadstool walks off and the mice move in, and the old stump becomes a house. The wobble is their first real disagreement, about the house.

### 3.0 A house for winter (the stump house quest)
*Opens the first time "Hollow out the old stump" can be started (usually week two, so often before 3.1). It starts a quest, "A proper home", shown at the top of the Journal with four steps that tick off as they are done.*

It was the first cold morning. Not very cold: just enough that Hazel's breath made a little cloud, and Wendel's toes found hers under the blanket and were told to go away.

Hazel sat up and looked at the canvas, and the moss roof, and the big old stump the shelter leaned against.

"Wendel," she said. "That stump is hollow."

"Mm."

"A stump that size could be a house. A real one. With a door, and windows, and a chimney, before the winter comes."

Wendel was out of bed so fast he took the blanket with him. He had been waiting the whole of his life for somebody to say those words.

**The quest: A proper home**
- [ ] Hollow out the old stump
- [ ] Fit a plank wall and windows
- [ ] Raise the roof and chimney
- [ ] Move in (page 3.5)

**Button:** Let's build a house

### 3.1 Next door
*Opens when the land next door on the left (the couple's garden, tile 0,1) turns green.*

Hazel stood at the edge of the yard with her hands on her hips and looked at the patch next door, which had been grey and bare a week ago and was now, very definitely, green.

"A garden," she said. "A real one. Flowers by the path. A corner for the creatures. Somewhere to sit that isn't next to your woodpile."

"What's wrong with my woodpile?"

"Gerald lives in it."

There was only one problem. Right in the middle of her garden stood the biggest, reddest, most stubborn toadstool either of them had ever seen. Wendel pushed it, very politely. Hazel pulled it. It did not so much as wobble.

"Sorry, old fellow," said Wendel, patting the cap. "We don't mean to be rude. You're just in the way of the roses."

Neither of them noticed what drifted out of the trees just then, soft and green as new leaves, and settled beside the toadstool as if it had been waiting for exactly this. But you did.

**Button:** Hello there

### 3.2 The toadstool takes a walk
*Opens when the toadstool has finished walking to the garden's far corner, after the player casts "Give the toadstool legs".*

It is not every day you see a toadstool get up.

First it shivered. Then four knobbly root legs wriggled out from under its cap, tested the ground one at a time, and stood it up with a creak. It looked about, the way you do when you've slept too long. Then it set off, wobble, wobble, wobble, to the far corner of the garden, and sat down there with a sigh of great satisfaction.

Wendel's pipe fell out of his mouth. Hazel picked it up and put it back in without looking.

"Did you do that?" Wendel asked her.

"No," said Hazel, and looked straight at you. "But I know who did."

**Button:** Just a little magic

### 3.3 Somebody's moved in
*Opens the day after the toadstool's little door and window appear, when the mouse family moves in (`flags.miceIn`). If the field mouse was never helped out of the bottle, the second paragraph starts "A field mouse who had been watching from the long grass" instead.*

On the morning after the toadstool grew its door, there was a tiny line of washing strung between its stalk and a blade of grass.

The field mouse they had once helped out of a bottle had moved in, and brought her family: one husband, very polite, and three small mice who had already found the seed bed. Hazel let them off, the first time.

"That's our first neighbours," said Wendel, delighted.

"That's our first tenants," said Hazel. "They can pay rent in being sweet." They are very good at it.

And look: wherever a creature makes a home, the land takes heart. The grass round the toadstool was greener by lunchtime.

**Button:** Welcome home, mice

### 3.4 Big plans
*Opens when "Hollow out the old stump" is finished.*

The old stump was hollow now, with room inside for a bed, a hearth and a table. Wendel had drawn the plans. There were a lot of them.

There was a tower, for the stars. A round room at the top with a telescope. A workbench in the kitchen, "so I can be near you", with all his bits and pieces on the shelf where the plates should go.

Hazel looked at the plans for a long time. Then she said, "No," and it was not her happy no.

Wendel looked at the plans too, and then at her face, and understood at once. "You wanted somewhere for us," he said. "Not somewhere for my bits and pieces." They ate supper without talking much, but it was a thinking quiet, not a cross one. In the morning, she found him already outside, drawing again. This time it was a small lean-to against the side of the house, all his own, with a workbench and a hook for every tool and a little window that looked at the stars.

"Your mess can live in there," said Hazel, and kissed the top of his head. "And the spare room stays spare."

"What for?"

"You never know," said Hazel.

**Button:** Back to work

### 3.5 Over the threshold
*Opens when "Raise the roof and chimney" is finished and the stump house is done. Closes chapter three; the first traveller follows as now.*

The last shingle went on at sunset. The chimney drew on the first try. The round windows glowed.

Wendel insisted on doing it properly. He scooped Hazel up at the doorstep, took one grand step, caught his foot on the doorstep he had laid himself, and they both went through the door in a heap and lay on the new floor laughing until they cried.

"Welcome home, my love," said Wendel, from somewhere under her skirts.

"Welcome home, you great lump," said Hazel.

Neither of them noticed the little board over the door yet, painted in Hazel's best lettering. THE GNOMESTEAD, it said, with a tiny snail underneath, taking the last letter very slowly.

That night, for the first time, they slept under a real roof in a real house, The Gnomestead, in a valley that was learning to be green again. And far down the old track, a small lantern was bobbing up the hill. Somebody else was coming.

**Button:** The end of chapter three

## The Wood spirit's lessons

The Wood spirit comes five times in the first month, once each time the player's magic grows, and teaches one new thing on a greeting card (small card size, not a full story page). Its one big lesson, repeated gently each time: **magic is for asking, not pushing**; it only helps what is already trying to live. It floats beside the thing it is teaching about until tapped. The gnomes cannot see it, though Hazel sometimes looks right at it.

Whichever lesson comes first gets this opening line: *"Something soft and green drifts out of the trees, like a handful of new leaves that forgot to fall. It looks at you as if it has always known you were there."*

### Lesson one: Coax a sprout (second day of visits, magic level 2)
*It floats over the seed bed.*

"You feel the light you gather, little spirit? That is your magic. It cannot make a thing grow. Nothing can. But it can whisper to a seed that is already trying, and tell it to hurry, because somebody is hungry."

**New in your magic:** Coax a sprout.

### Lesson two: Call soft rain (third day, magic level 3)
*It floats over the spring pool.*

"The clouds go over this hill without stopping. They think nothing lives here. Ask them nicely to look down, and they might. Never ask for a storm. A garden needs a drink, not a bath."

**New in your magic:** Call soft rain.

### Lesson three: Give the toadstool legs (when the garden opens)
*It floats round the big toadstool. Replaces the v50 card text.*

"Big, isn't it? And very settled. You could push it all day and it wouldn't care. But ask it kindly, give it a little light, and it will find its own legs. It might even like the corner better."

**New in your magic:** Give the toadstool legs.

### Lesson four: Wake the land (fifth day, magic level 4)
*It floats at the edge of the healed ground, where the land next door is still a pencil sketch (the sketch look comes in a later chat; until then, at the misty edge).*

"See out there, where the world is only drawn and not yet painted? It is not dead. It is asleep, and dreaming of what it was. When the gnomes have done their part, you can wake it a little sooner. Only a little. Waking up is not something you rush."

**New in your magic:** Wake the land.

### Lesson five: A bright fire (eighth day, magic level 5)
*It comes at dusk and floats by the campfire.*

"Your light is strong now, stronger than mine was at your age. There is nothing new to teach you today, only this: you will find light in the dark, in wisps and dew, and you will also find it every time you are kind. Help the small ones when they are in trouble. That is the brightest magic there is."

"I'll be about. When the valley needs something new, I'll come and show you."

**No new spell.** The journal gets a Wood spirit page, and the spirit can turn up later to teach any new special spell the same way.

## The land is waking (greening rule)

Only living things bring the land back. Building gives no green on its own, however lovely. Growing things do, and so does a creature moving into a home the gnomes made, because that is life coming back too.

| What happens | Green it brings |
| --- | --- |
| The spring runs again | 1 |
| Each harvest from the seed bed | 1 |
| Window box, herb corner | 1 each |
| Little oak planted, berry bush, wildflowers | 2 each |
| A creature moves into a gnome-made home (nest box, leaf pile, bee house, pond, burrow, the mouse family's toadstool) | 1 each |
| Anything built (fence, bench, roof, lantern, birdbath, compost, house, bridge) | 0 |

Three cards mark the big changes (they replace the `explain` text in `CONTENT.landStages`). Each opens once.

**First green.** You would have to kneel to see it. A haze of green over the bare brown earth, so faint that Hazel thought it was her eyes. It isn't. Every living thing the gnomes grow here teaches the soil to grow again. The spring brought the water back, and each crop leaves a little more life behind. Keep growing things, and it will spread.

**Young meadow.** One morning there was grass round the house. Not a haze any more: real grass, with clover in it, and the first daisies, and a magpie strutting about on it as if he had planted it himself. The ground remembers now. Plant things that stay, like trees and bushes and flowers, and the woods will start to come home.

**Young woodland.** The saplings are taller than a gnome now. You have to look up at them. The creatures who left long ago are finding their way back, one by one, following the green. This is not a clearing any more. It is the beginning of a wood.

**Small notes when a creature moves in** (toast + "While you were away"):
- "The robins have moved into the nest box. The grass by the fence looks greener this morning."
- "Something prickly has moved into the leaf pile. The ground round it is waking up."
- "A frog has moved into the pond, and the land is the better for it."
- "The mice have moved into the toadstool. Wherever someone makes a home, the land takes heart."

## All done for now

Once a visit, when both gnomes are busy (or nothing more can be started) and every pick-up lying about has been collected, the visit gently closes: one gnome looks up and says a line, a small paper label reads **All done for now**, and the view eases out a little to show the whole yard. Nothing is locked: chatting, little finds and fiddling stay open. `{time}` is the real time their jobs finish.

**Both busy, daytime**
- Hazel: "That's us busy till about {time}, love. Off you go. We'll be here."
- Wendel: "Nothing more to do but wait and work. Come back after {time} and see what we've made."
- Hazel: "Go on, shoo. A watched seed bed never sprouts."

**Nothing more to start (waiting on materials or time)**
- Wendel: "We'll need a few more bits before the next job. The land always leaves something out. Come and look later."
- Hazel: "That's all we can do today. It's enough. A little each day, that's the trick."

**Evening**
- Hazel: "Kettle's on, the fire's lit, and we're stopping. Come and say goodnight later if you like."
- Wendel: "I'm putting my feet up. Don't tell anyone I said that before six."

**Night**
- Hazel, whispering: "He's asleep already. Come back in the morning, there'll be dew."
- Wendel, yawning: "Night, little spirit. Mind the wisps on the way out."

**After a big day** (a story page opened this visit)
- Hazel: "What a day. I think we've earned a sit down, the three of us."

## The five tidy-ups, in story words

1. **One yard light (parked for now).** v51 takes out the little fireside lamp stump and shelves "Mend the old lantern", so the first month has no lantern. When a yard light comes back (with the parked lamp posts) it is just one, and lights along the paths are Juniper the lamplighter's gift to the village. The glow-worm lanterns job (`wormlantern`) goes either way.
2. **Wendel's workshop.** *Raise Wendel's workshop lean-to* (appears after page 3.4): "A lean-to of his own against the side of the house, with a hook for every tool and a little window for the stars. His mess lives in here now. Hazel is delighted." The workbench goes inside it once Tobias's planks arrive. The spare room stays spare.
3. **The spring gets a stone ring.** *Ring the spring with stones:* "Flat stones round the spring pool, so the edges don't crumble and the birds have somewhere to stand. Not a well. Wells are for villages." The village well belongs to the Fernsbys.
4. **Real crops.** Carrots first (pages 1.4 and 2.1), then beans, which need *Raise the bean poles*: "For the beans, which have strong opinions about climbing." Wendel's one enormous pumpkin seed waits for the crops chat to give it its moment. Harvests go into the pantry.
5. **Compost.** *Start a compost heap:* "Peelings, leaves and patience. Dug into the seed bed, it makes the soil rich: one extra seed every harvest." The dew pond is fed by a trickle from the spring instead.
