- **Rounds one to five** only survive as summaries. Bryce's own words from those rounds could not be found, so no rule quotes them.
- The pretend player cannot see pictures, so it cannot judge little finds, animal visits or how a scene looks in motion. Those were only looked at in stills.
- The game's name was checked against other games with one quick web search only.

## Step 3. What the review found

### Real bugs (all fixed in v106)
1. **The story could stop for good.** The bench by the fire is an optional job, but the story page after it would not open without it, and every later page waited behind that one. A player who never built the bench stayed on page 5 of 14 forever: no house, no neighbours, no festival, with Jobs saying "when the story gets there (a page each morning)", which was not true. Now the bench page simply waits for the bench and the story goes on without it.
2. **Two story pages spoke of things that might not exist:** the bench (on the little oak page) and "the doorstep he had laid himself" (the doorstep is optional). Both now only say what is true.
3. **A box of darker paper round the land** when you pinch right out, from summer on, once the valley is bigger than a few patches.
4. **"Dew" was still asked for** by neighbours in eleven places after dew became water, while the store only shows water.
5. **The diary said a visitor "ate the berries"** when it was a carrot in the dish.
6. **A phone left open past midnight did not count the new day** until the page was loaded afresh. One spell lesson waits on the day count.
7. **Hazel still "walked softly behind" Wendel** on the spring page, which round seven note 5 asked to be taken out.

### Things Bryce would likely complain about, not yet reported
- **The empty stretch (the main one, see Step 4).** After a keen first two days nothing new can start for about a week, because every job waits for "one story page each morning".
- **The home yard is fully grown by the second day** for a keen player (day five to seven for a normal one). The slow greening he loves is over almost at once. Rule 8.
- **Story pages arrive long after the thing happened.** A keen player finishes the garden and the pond on day one and reads about it roughly nine mornings later. Rules 2 and 10.
- **Day-count words** in a game with no fixed length: "Our first week, or near enough" (Hazel's diary), "A week ago" and Mabel's "It is a month this week". Rule 12.
- **Some neighbour stories lean on old stereotypes.** Mabel's quest has Wendel forget an anniversary; several asks have Hazel doing the cooking, knitting and minding of children. Rule 11.
- **The name board is mentioned as already painted** on the moving-in page, while "paint a name board" is still on the wish list afterwards. Rules 2 and 10.
- **"Shelf full" tags cover the names of things** on the store shelves once they fill. Rule 5 (readable text).
- **Lots of little white flames with haloes** lie about a large late garden. They may read as glowing. Rule 15. (Seen in stills only.)
- **Counters that only go up**: "501 grown so far" on the seed bed by day 100. Harmless, but it turns a garden into a tally.

### Phone performance
On the test machine the game held full speed (about 60 pictures a second) on every day from 1 to 100, including the 25-patch valley. The work per picture roughly doubles between a bare day-one yard and a full yard, and is highest around day 14 when the yard is busiest; it does not keep climbing after that. A tablet-sized screen was the only one to drop (about 38 a second). Memory stayed small. None of this was on a real iPhone, so treat it as "no warning signs", not "proven smooth".

### Does the garden survive?
- The save is small (2 KB on day one, 46 KB on day 100) and nothing in it grows without limit.
- A week away and a month away both open cleanly: no errors, no scolding, one story page waiting, crops ready.
- Opening it five times in a day is safe; nothing is counted twice.
- **The real risk is outside the game.** The garden lives only in the phone's browser storage. There is no backup. Clearing Safari's website data, or a new phone, loses it for good. Safari can also clear storage for a web page that has not been opened for a while if it is not on the Home Screen. v106 now asks the phone to keep the save, which helps on some phones and is harmless on the rest; a proper "save my garden to a file" button is one of the questions.
- If a save ever became unreadable the game would quietly start a fresh garden over it. Very unlikely, but worth guarding before release.

### Pile 4. Before release (the stranger check). Leave alone unless cheap.
First-time players with nobody to explain:
- The welcome is a long page of words before anything happens. Watch one stranger play the first five minutes before deciding anything.
- "You are the heart of the wood" is said once; a stranger may not grasp that they never do the physical work.
- A few places still use emoji (the How to play list, for one); these look different on every make of phone.
- A fifth of taps did nothing in Bryce's own log, and he knows the game.

Other phones and screens:
- Small phones (320 wide): everything fits, but the Journal's contents page is cut off at the bottom.
- Tablets: works, noticeably slower. A phone on its side: works, but menus cover the whole screen.
- Only tested with an Android-type browser here. Needs a real iPhone, an older iPhone and an Android phone.
- Pinch-to-zoom for the page is switched off, which people with poor eyesight rely on. Menu text size should become a setting.

Keeping it going for strangers:
- No backup of the garden (question 5). The Home Screen copy and the Safari copy of the game are two separate gardens, which will confuse people.
- It does not work with no signal, and it has no proper app icon or name for the Home Screen.
- The lettering is fetched from Google each time. For a public release it should be packed inside the game (privacy rules in some countries, and it works offline).
- The play log and the "concern" button are built for Bryce. A public version needs them off, or a plain sentence saying what is kept and where.
- A release needs a short privacy note and a credits page.

Too close to something that exists (needs a human decision, not a code fix):
- **The art.** The painting prompts name one real book, "The Secret World of the Gnomes", as the style to copy, and one doc describes Wendel as "like David the gnome". Red pointed hats and blue smocks are old folklore and free to use, but a look that was steered at one living illustrator's book is worth showing to someone who knows the book before any sale. At the least, the prompts should describe the style in plain words (brown ink line, soft watercolour, muted earthy colours) and stop naming the book.
- **The paintings were made with an AI image tool.** Some shops and some buyers want that said. Decide how to say it.
- **The name.** One quick search found no game called exactly "Gnome Life", but there are near neighbours ("Gnome Homes", "Gnomes Garden"). Do a proper name and trademark check before release.
- **Names in the story** (Wendel, Hazel, Mabel, Tobias, the Pebbleworths, Gerald the beetle, The Gnomestead) were not found to copy anything, but this was not searched properly.
