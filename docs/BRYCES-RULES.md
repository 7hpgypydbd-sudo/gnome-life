# Bryce's rules

The patterns behind Bryce's play notes, written down so every future change can be checked against them before he ever sees it. Worked out in the full review of 2026-11-16 (see `docs/REVIEW-2026-11.md`) from the notes tracker, the round six to thirteen notes, and the summaries of rounds one to five.

**How to use this page.** Before any change that can be seen or read in the game goes to Bryce, go down this list and ask of the change: "which rule would he quote at me?" Fix that first. When a new note arrives that fits no rule here, add a rule (with his words) in the same chat.

Words in quotes are Bryce's own, from the notes. Rounds one to five only survive as summaries, so nothing here quotes them. A rule marked **(guess)** is my reading and has not been said by him in so many words.

## 1. Everything is there for a reason, and the reason can be seen
If something appears, changes or gets done, the player must be able to see why.
- "The barrel does not make sense: too small to be a loggers barrel, not close enough to the roof to collect water."
- "The log also changed orientation for no reason."
- "That is not a tile greening that is dead wood appearing for no reason."
- Moss on the roof came back three times (rounds ten, eleven, thirteen) until it had a visible reason (the leak).
- "We chop one stump but no this one?"
- Dew as a thing to collect "makes no sense" beside a spring.

## 2. Words must match what is on the screen, right now
A line that names the wrong gnome, the wrong moment, or something that has not happened yet is a bug.
- A card said "on planting day" while "it is still the first day".
- The spirit called itself "the spirit from the first day" after an hour of play: "wrong wording".
- "Wendel is done building but it looks unfinished."
- A message that something grew "appears randomly, confusing, annoying; also untrue".
- Cards that say the same thing twice ("double up") count too.

## 3. Neither gnome stands idle, and there is always something to do
The most repeated note of all, and the thing he loves most when it works.
- "Hazel can't do anything again. Why aren't the little jobs available?"
- "Wendel was watering and Hazel had nothing to do."
- Loved: "always having something to do"; "little jobs let him grind resources; plenty of makes".
- Day two with one job and some makes "feels limited".
- The work is shared: "Wendel has no things of his own" was a complaint, and so was Hazel getting every early job.

## 4. Waits are short, especially early
- "It shouldn't take 30 mins to plant the oak sapling way way too long 10 mins."
- "The pumpkin shouldn't take 24 hours to grow; far too long."
- The first neighbour at about fifteen real days was "too long".
- No magic "after a whole first day of play" was a complaint.

## 5. Less text, and one voice at a time
- "Bombarded with many pop-ups at once again; they didn't make sense."
- Several pop-ups "overlap, vanish too fast".
- "All of this is a confusing mess" (the old Magic page).
- "Too many job flags just appeared on the map": flags only for jobs that move the game on.
- Even he, who knows how it works, "finds it complex and slightly confusing".
- The other half of this rule: the text that stays must be big enough. "The text in the whole game is too small."

## 6. The player is the heart of the wood; the gnomes do the physical work
Anything that looks like the player's hand doing a gnome's job is wrong, and magic must be clear, visible and have a point.
- Seed in the dish "looks like the player does it" while the carrot is Hazel's.
- "ALL magic needs rethinking": each spell now has a cost, a reason and something you can see happen.

## 7. Things sit properly in the world
On the ground, the right size next to a gnome, not on top of each other, the right way round in front of or behind each other, and all in the same painted style.
- "The house looks like it's floating; there shouldn't be a shadow under it."
- "The wood pile looks like it's floating."
- "The shoe scraper is too big and in the way and the doormat is misplaced due to it."
- "The clothesline needs loving and so does the wood pile" (crowded together).
- A stump whose "art doesn't match the other art".
- Stand-in drawings made in code are never good enough for something a month-one player meets.

## 8. Finished things look finished, and progress shows
- The work corner "finished but looks unfinished".
- The greening meter: "Ugh, I don't know. Brainstorm and rethink required." (still open: he wants a clear way to see how green the yard and valley are).
- Loved: "green grass slowly spreading" on the home yard.

## 9. Taps are forgiving and things are easy to find
- "Takes too many taps for things to happen on the map, and the touch isn't accurate."
- The seed bed took four taps. In his round-thirteen log, 19 of 129 taps did nothing.

## 10. The story comes in the right order and no scene is missed
- The rabbit raid has to be a scene you watch, then a prompt: "it all happened too quick".
- A first meeting with an animal is a scene; the camera goes to it.
- Story pages should not tell you about something you have not seen yet, or long after it happened **(the "long after" half is a guess; he has not played far enough to meet it)**.

## 11. Tone: gentle, shared, no stereotypes
- Hazel is practical, never sceptical, never hen-pecking. Wendel is a gentle dreamer, never thoughtless or the cause of trouble.
- Avoid gender stereotypes; jobs go to whichever gnome is free.
- Things belong to both of them ("One day this will be our garden").

## 12. No day counts, no fixed length
"Game length is not fixed, so no line may name a day count or 'first day'; reword to match what actually happened." Never plan to a target day.

## 13. Only living things bring the green, and gently
Greening comes from growing things, small things first ("the first stage should be smaller things: grasses, sprouts, small flowers"), and unopened land stays pencil on paper.

## 14. Details and small surprises are wanted
He loves "the mouse hanging around while the gnomes work", "the gnomes are eating breakfast" in the morning, little finds, the snail hidden in every painting. Animals are rare until they are friends ("too many animal appearances right now").

## 15. The look: an old storybook, nothing glows
Parchment menus, ink and watercolour, never clay or toy-like, and nothing glows.

## 16. Leave the loved things alone **(guess, but a safe one)**
Always something to do; the first day not being overwhelming; the extra patch on day one; the slow grass spread; the stump and the spring; breakfast; the mouse; little finds. A change that touches one of these gets shown to him first.

## 17. The garden must never be lost, and the play log holds everything
The size cap once dropped four hours of his play log. A lost garden would be far worse **(the garden half is a guess: it has not happened yet)**.
