## Rules for the garden (all three layouts)

1. Draw the finished garden first; every make gets a fixed spot from that picture (like the yard plan of 2026-10-26).
2. Keep open grass between things: at least a gnome's width, and a clear walk from the arch to every make.
3. Tall things (arch, foxgloves, mouse house, ferns) at the back or the sides; low things in front, so nothing hides a bed.
4. No random big pieces on the garden: no scattered boulders, big tufts, root tangles or logs, and at the map's edge only low living plants.
5. Greening comes in bit by bit like the home yard (Bryce loves that, note 33): the gnomes clear the rubbish, the player casts Wake the worms (note 22), grass creeps in, then the garden opens. Nothing big appears at first green (note 31).
6. The card says from the start that this is their garden-to-be, in both gnomes' words or none (note 39).

## The other patches when finished (looked at a garden with every job done)

They are messy for the same few reasons, so one tidy-up fixes most of them:
1. Every grown-over patch, and the home yard at its last stage, uses the same "woodland" ground: lumpy moss cushions on dark soil with leaves. It is the busiest thing on the screen everywhere (Bryce's note about busy final ground).
2. Orange pine-needle mats (ground patch 05) are scattered as decoration and left over from the mulching jobs on the creature and nursery patches. They read as rugs.
3. Big tangled root stumps and logs are placed along the outer edge of the map when a patch greens. That is what popped up on the rubbish heap patch at first green (note 31).
4. Random big stickers land on top of planned spots: the big grey mossy boulder in the middle of the garden, bright lime moss cushions.
5. Neighbours' lots add little "woodland corner" clumps on top of the home, farm and wishes, so they feel crowded too.
